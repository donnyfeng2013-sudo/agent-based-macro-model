ABM2 Test Run 1

The first full run of the model, over 25 annual periods. Hiring and firing are not built yet, so firms cannot change output. All price and demand adjustment therefore runs through inventories and prices. Profits are paid out as a placeholder dividend so money flows back to households.

Setup

Firms
1,234 in 68 sectors (3-digit NAICS), from company financials; headcount compressed 1,000 : 1
Households
13,660, of which 5% start unemployed
Periods
25 years, seed 1
Job matching
active no vacancies are created yet, so nobody is hired or fired
Production
active fixed input coefficients (BEA use table), markup pricing adjusted by inventory gap
Consumption
active linear expenditure system; households switch to cheaper firms
Income
placeholder fixed wages; all positive profit paid as dividends in proportion to wealth
Parameters
price cut 0.1 · price rise 0.05 · switching 1.0 · MPC wealth 0.27
Units
money in thousand USD (model scale); quantities in units at initial price 1

What the run shows

  1. The accounting holds. Household wealth plus firm liquidity is 18.07m in every period, so no money is created or lost.
  2. The circular flow roughly balances at first. For the first nine years household spending stays within 4% of income (wages plus dividends). For the first seven years the median price stays within 2.5% of 1.
  3. Quantities cannot adjust. Output is fixed at 7.86m units, and units sold exceed output in 20 of 25 periods, so imbalances accumulate as inventory gaps.
  4. Demand is not capped by capacity. From period 1 one firm owes 100 times its annual output, and 300 times by period 3. That backlog then shrinks by exactly one year of output per period, so the firm has lost its customers but still sees a huge price signal.
  5. Backlogs turn into inflation that spreads. Backlogged firms raise prices every period, and markup pricing passes those prices into their customers' unit costs. From period 6 the 90th-percentile price roughly doubles each year. By period 25 the median price is 78 and the mean is 9×10²³.
  6. Losses and profits both run away. Firms buying from those suppliers run unlimited negative liquidity (−355m by period 25). The suppliers' profits flow to households as dividends, so wealth and spending inflate with prices. The first firm with a non-positive price appears in period 27.

Time series

Next steps

  • Let firms change output through vacancies, hiring and firing (Phase 5), so that surpluses and backlogs are cleared by quantities as well as prices.
  • Deliver only what is in stock, so that unfilled orders do not become permanent backlogs that keep pushing prices up.
  • Bound the price adjustment, for example a multiplicative rule that cannot go negative and a cap on the inventory signal.
  • Limit how far a firm's liquidity can go negative with a credit limit, or exit.
